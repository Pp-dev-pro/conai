02 Using the app: 8 steps

Every quantity is in kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m³), like a batching sheet. Ranges come from the training dataset; values outside them are less reliable.

Step 1: Add Cement

kg per m³ | Dataset range: 102 to 540

Cement binds the aggregates and is the strongest single driver of strength.

Step 2: Add Blast Furnace Slag

kg per m³ | Dataset range: 0 to 359.4

A steel-making by-product that replaces part of the cement. Enter 0 if not used.

Step 3: Add Fly Ash

kg per m³ | Dataset range: 0 to 200.1

Fine grey powder from power plants that saves cement and reduces heat. Enter 0 if absent.

Step 4: Add Water

kg per m³ | Dataset range: 121.8 to 247

Hydrates the cement and makes the mix workable. More water lowers strength (correlation −0.29).

Step 5: Add Superplasticizer

kg per m³ | Dataset range: 0 to 32.2

Improves workability so less water is needed. Enter 0 if none is used.

Step 6: Add Age

days | Dataset range: 1 to 365

Strength grows fast early and then slows, so 7 and 28 days give very different answers.

Step 7: Coarse and fine aggregate

Coarse aggregate (gravel): 801 to 1145 kg/m³. Fine aggregate (sand): 594 to 992.6 kg/m³. They make up most of the volume and must be consistent with cement and water.


Step 8: Tap Predict Strength

Fill all eight fields (use 0 for slag, fly ash or superplasticizer if absent), check units, and tap the purple button on the Ready to predict card.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong unit: litres of water or bags of cement instead of kg per m³.
  • Empty field: slag, fly ash and superplasticizer must be 0, not blank.
  • Values outside the range (for example age 500 days): the answer is a guess.
  • Batch vs cubic metre: a 0.5 m³ batch ticket must be doubled.
  • Wrong age: days since casting, not since another pour started.
